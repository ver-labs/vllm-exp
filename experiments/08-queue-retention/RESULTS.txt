Experiment 08: queue-informed retention of free cached prefix blocks
======================================================================

Completed on the Mac Metal GPU with the pinned Qwen3-0.6B model.
Three policies: upstream vLLM, existing cumulative LFU, queue-aware retention.
All use FCFS, exact 16-token prefix matching, synchronous chunked prefill,
identical page budgets, request manifests, arrivals and fixed output counts.
Earlier partial-prefix and skip/continue changes are disabled.
Each profile has a separate diagnostic per policy and 3 untraced timed rounds
with rotated policy order and a fresh server for every run: 24 runs total.

Interpretation and limits
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The targeted diagnostic shows queue information can preserve a prefix that
both recency and cumulative frequency lose before the first queued return.
Targeted queue median completion was 9.82% lower than stock, but 1.84% higher
than LFU. Original A-Y queue median was 1.82% lower than stock, but 2.26%
higher than LFU. Queue beat stock in 2/3 targeted and 3/3 A-Y rounds, and beat
LFU in 0/3 targeted and 2/3 A-Y rounds. Medians do not establish superiority.
A-Y retained 2432 tokens under every policy and queue changed no upstream
allocation choice in its diagnostic, so it shows no additional reuse gain.
Stock first-round times were unusually high: 34.157s in targeted and 17.562s
in A-Y. They remain included; no timing outlier is silently discarded.
This does not imply a general throughput gain. All paired timings are
reported above; 3 repeats and machine variability limit causal speed claims.
The targeted workload deliberately exposes this retention opportunity; the
original A-Y workload is the generalization control. First-text time includes
waiting, and candidate completion includes remaining blocker time after release.
Cache-hit token counts are not FLOP or elapsed-time measurements.
Answer agreement is an exact-text comparison, not a semantic quality score.
Diagnostic selection CPU timings include probes/bucketing, exclude event writing,
and are not used to rank performance; timed runs have tracing disabled.
No extra KV capacity, approximate middle-block reuse, or queue reordering is used.

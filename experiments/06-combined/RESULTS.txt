Experiment 06: experiments 02, 04 and 05 enabled together
==========================================================

Completed on 2026-10-21 with Qwen3-0.6B on the Mac Metal GPU. The combined
variant enables frequency-aware eviction (02), exact interior-prefix indexing
(04), and bounded unchunked skip/continue scheduling (05) at the same time.
Native FCFS with upstream eviction, 16-token prefix matching and all three study
switches off is the baseline. Both variants use the editable vLLM source, the
same model, prompts, arrival patterns, seed, output limits and per-profile
serving settings. Every run starts a fresh server. Experiment03's approximate
non-prefix repair is off. All 24 runs completed: one traced diagnostic and
three timed matched pairs per variant on each of three profiles.

The three profiles exercise distinct bottlenecks. A-Y uses the original 25
concurrent prompts with 3 running slots and 96 KV pages. Targeted uses 13 long
then 12 short prompts with 8 slots and 256 pages. Temporal uses 42 sequential
hot-prefix, cold-scan and return requests with 3 slots and 32 pages. All use
unchunked prefill, so skip/continue can be enabled throughout. A-Y and targeted
use a 512-token batch/context limit; temporal uses 384. Physical pages hold 16
tokens. Therefore these are new matched comparisons, not historical cross-run
comparisons with experiments 02, 04 or 05, some of which used chunked prefill.

Median over three timed runs           Baseline       Combined
A-Y completion                          18.512 s      18.410 s
A-Y candidate cached tokens/run           2432           2563
Targeted completion                     12.932 s      10.060 s
Targeted output throughput              123.72/s      159.04/s
Targeted candidate cached tokens/run        400            549
Temporal full batch                     12.965 s      12.603 s
Temporal hot-return first text          170.98 ms      83.55 ms
Temporal hot-return cached tokens          16            277

These medians alone overstate certainty. Paired completion-time changes were:
  A-Y:      -2.17%, +1.83%, -9.54% (combined faster in 2/3 pairs).
  Targeted: -22.67%, -2.45%, +28.00% (combined faster in 2/3 pairs).
  Temporal: -13.35%, +0.08%, +46.79% (combined faster in 1/3 pairs).
The large swings affected both variants. Three pairs do not establish a stable
overall speedup. The hot-return cache-hit gain was stable in all runs: 16 tokens
per probe in baseline, 277 in combined. This shows retention and finer prefix
reuse together, not a throughput guarantee. The 277 versus experiment02's 272
under its different configuration suggests partial-prefix reuse adds five
tokens, but this run did not isolate that contribution with an ablation.

The targeted diagnostic logged 28 skip events in combined versus zero in
baseline; baseline stopped at an oversized waiting prompt six times versus two
stops in combined. Original A-Y and temporal diagnostics logged no skips. A-Y
candidate cache hits rose by 131 tokens/run in all three timed runs. Targeted
hits rose by 149. Temporal hot-return hits were 16 versus 277 for every one of
the three probes in every timed run. All diagnostic traces confirmed the intended
eviction and scheduling switches, source checkout, unchunked prefill and zero
preemptions. Cached-block evictions occurred in all profiles.

The targeted workload retains a substantial fairness cost:
  Long-prompt median first text: 2.580 -> 6.194 s (about 2.4x slower).
  Short-prompt median first text: 7.770 -> 3.865 s (about 50% faster).
These are medians of each run's group median. The combined policy's bounds do not
free running slots held by shorter requests. Reporting only whole-batch time
would hide this cost.

Successful output-token counts matched for each profile: 1984 per A-Y run,
2112 per targeted run, and 336 per temporal run. All manifests matched within
each profile and every request succeeded. Exact answer matches across paired
timed runs were A-Y 28/28, 28/28, 26/28; targeted 28/33, 28/33, 27/33;
temporal 42/42 in every pair. Answers were not semantically scored. Different
scheduling or prefill shapes can alter low-precision generation; this experiment
does not isolate the cause of each text difference or establish quality parity.

Conclusion: all three changes can operate together. Their mechanisms activated
on the profiles designed for them, including stable extra prefix reuse and
short-request latency gains. Combined batch speed was inconsistent, while long
prompts paid a clear latency penalty. Keep this an opt-in research result; do
not claim a general faster scheduler from these runs.

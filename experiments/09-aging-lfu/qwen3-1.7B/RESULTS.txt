Experiment 09: aging frequency-based prefix-cache retention
=================================================================

Completed on Qwen3-1.7B with native Mac Metal GPU execution.
Stock, cumulative LFU and aging LFU; no queue retention, partial reuse, or
skip/continue. Same model, requests, page capacity and output counts within
each comparison. Decay interval 64 physical block allocations was fixed
before measurements; it was not retuned after seeing results.
Halving is global and synchronized before selection, including active scores.
Only zero-reference pages are candidates; normal allocation remains possible.
One traced diagnostic and three untraced fresh-server timing rounds per
policy/profile, with rotated launch order:24 serving runs in total.
All profiles use 32 physical16-token pages (31 usable), context384, batch128,
one running slot, sequential requests, FCFS, synchronous chunked prefill,
temperature0, seed0, ignore EOS, eight outputs/request, one-token warmup.

Interpretation and limits
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Adaptation is the first probe cycle followed by another full-hit probe.
Null means no such consecutive pair within the measured window; it is not
an extrapolation to infinite time. The stable profile has no traffic switch,
so use its hit series to assess the retention tradeoff rather than adaptation.
Stock and cumulative LFU can reuse B during consecutive B-training requests;
the probe series measures survival through the subsequent cold scans.
Cache reuse, adaptation speed and wall-clock speed are distinct measurements.
Three timing rounds are exploratory; preserve the ranges and paired results.
Exact text agreement does not measure semantic quality. This is a fixed
small-pool experiment on one model, not a production/general speed claim.
Source/model revisions, file hashes, summaries, CSV and validation are saved.
Large raw logs remain under results/aging-lfu/Qwen--Qwen3-1.7B.
All experimental servers stopped. Reproduction:PLAN.txt.

Interpretation — A100 benchmark, 26 October 2026
================================================

No throughput speedup over stock is established in this fixed test. All 24
candidate-versus-stock throughput intervals either cross zero or are negative.
The most consistent policy effect is worse first-token latency with partial
reuse, despite caching 15 additional prompt tokens on eligible shared hits.

Partial reuse
-------------
On partial_reuse, native cached-token counts rise from 4,608 to 6,048 across
96 requests (48 to 63 per prompt, +31.25%). On popularity_shift they rise from
3,600 to 4,710. The output work and output token IDs remain identical. Yet
partial/combined p50 TTFT is consistently worse on these two workloads for both
models: approximately 12–16%, with all five paired repeats losing. Absolute
TTFT rises roughly from 22–23 ms to 25–26 ms. This supports the narrower result
"extra cache hits did not pay off here." It does not isolate a cause: lookup,
bookkeeping, copies, and changed execution shapes are plausible contributors;
this measurement contains no component-level timing to attribute the cost.

Throughput / end-to-end latency
-------------------------------
For 0.6B, partial and combined lose throughput and p95 E2E in all five repeats
on every profile. Throughput median changes are roughly -0.5% to -1.5%. For
1.7B, the throughput effects are small and their intervals span zero. The
candidate-off controls also show run variation; they remain essential to
interpreting small source-versus-stock differences. Ratio-of-median numbers
can differ in sign from median paired effects; CSV and figures use the latter.

Aging
-----
Aging alone does not establish a throughput improvement on either model.
Native cache/preemption counts are identical to stock in every profile, so
this workload does not demonstrate a retention benefit. One small isolated
p95-latency result (1.7B unique_control, +0.44%) has a positive repeat-bootstrap
interval, but there is no corresponding established throughput gain and no
multiplicity correction across the many exploratory comparisons. This is not
evidence of a broad improvement. Half-life four allocations is an aggressive
correctness-stress value, not an optimized policy. These results do not test
all aging parameters or traffic patterns.

What this establishes
---------------------
50 fresh engines, five balanced paired repeats, 150 timed profiles, 14,400
requests and 460,800 actual output tokens completed. All generated token IDs
matched stock across arms and repeats; the full archive and all 177 members
were SHA256 verified. Independent local analysis reproduced the cluster JSON,
CSV and text summary byte for byte. Earlier frozen research packages are
unchanged. All comparisons used the same allocated GPU UUID.

These are synthetic 64-token prompts and 32-token outputs at concurrency three,
with a deliberately tiny 16-page KV pool, BF16 batch-invariant eager execution
on a full-size A100 40 GB MIG instance. This is achieved token-ID engine
performance; it excludes HTTP, model loading, detokenization and requests
waiting outside the admission window. It is not peak A100 capacity, default
vLLM performance, a production SLO study, or a general result about all KV reuse.

Next research decision
----------------------
Do not claim a combined-policy speedup from this experiment. The next separate
protocol should test the reuse break-even point across prompt lengths, saved
prefix lengths, KV budgets and offered loads, with component profiling to
explain overhead. Aging needs traffic where eviction choices actually differ.
No settings were changed or selectively rerun after these timings were seen.

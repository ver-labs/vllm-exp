Attention-copy optimization: interpretation
==========================================

The guarded attention change improves throughput and p95 total request latency
over stock on both models in every tested workload. The additional benefit of
partial reuse over attention-only is not established. First-token latency does
not show a consistent improvement for attention-only; partial reuse remains
slower than stock for first-token latency on the shared-prefix workloads.

What changed
------------
The patch selects the ordinary causal FA2 path for guarded one-query paged GQA
by retaining right window zero. With one last-position query, this window has
the same attention semantics as the unrestricted right window. It bypasses
query-head regrouping and associated layout copies. Query bounds, model
weights, KV contents, cache policy and native binaries are unchanged. The
flag also applies during eligible decode steps, not only partial-hit prefill.
It is an experimental routing policy, not a universally optimal default.

Comparison with stock
---------------------
All percentages below are medians of five paired-repeat changes. Positive is
better: higher output throughput, lower p95 total latency. They are not ratios
of the separately reported absolute medians. Workload names are synthetic tests.

Isolating the contribution
--------------------------
Attention versus features-off controls establishes the gain independently of
cache policy: +5.66% to +7.72% throughput, all five repeats winning in every
cell. Partial+attention versus old partial gives +5.32% to +7.28%, again all
five winning in every cell. The implementation change helped the old partial
variant too. Cached-token and preemption counts match within all 60 paired
workload comparisons for these two contrasts.

Every interval for extra partial reuse spans zero. We therefore cannot claim
a cache-policy throughput improvement from the combined variant. The simpler
attention-only variant has the strongest current evidence.

First-token latency
-------------------
Attention-only median TTFT is approximately unchanged versus stock. Combined
partial+attention reduces the earlier partial variant's TTFT penalty, but
remains about 6–9% worse than stock on the two shared-prefix workloads. Decode
and total latency improve without establishing a general first-token gain.
The lookup/ingestion, publication and required KV-copy costs remain separate
optimization opportunities. These results do not identify them as the next
best change in every workload.

Correctness and mechanism checks
--------------------------------
48 BF16/FP16 attention cases pass against original FA2 and an FP32 reference,
with unchanged tolerances. Noncausal, sliding-window and alibi fallbacks pass.
The direct output-buffer attention test reduces clones from two to zero.
Both optimized variants pass the previous model logits/KV/copy/reset/cancel/
preemption lifecycle checks on both Qwen sizes. All 1,572 non-stock tested logit
vectors have exact value equality with the frozen stock references (torch.equal,
max absolute/RMS error zero; no new claim about raw signed-zero byte patterns).
All 14,400 timed benchmark output sequences match stock across arms/repeats.

Separate post-benchmark model traces confirm the mechanism on both sizes:
first-forward clones 112 -> 56 and tensor-copy kernels 141 -> 57. Those 56 clones
and 84 copy kernels were physically removed; other model copies and necessary
KV-cache copies remain. The traces were outside the timed benchmark and are
not included in speed estimates.

Measurement reliability and limits
----------------------------------
50 fresh engines, five balanced paired repeats, 150 timed profiles, 14,400
requests and 460,800 actual output tokens on one A100 40 GB full-size MIG
instance. Source/native/model/workload hashes, settings and policy flags were
verified. Independent local analysis exactly reproduces all five cluster
summary/CSV/text artifacts, including the isolated upgrade comparisons. Earlier
research packages remain unchanged. Exact executed source copies are preserved.

These are in-process token-ID measurements: 64-token synthetic prompts, 32-token
outputs, closed-loop concurrency three, 16 KV pages, BF16 batch-invariant FA2,
eager synchronous execution. Loading, detokenization, HTTP and waiting outside
admission are excluded. This does not establish peak A100 capacity, default
CUDA-graph behavior, long-context performance, production SLO capacity or an
optimal routing policy for all GQA models. Five-repeat intervals are coarse,
and comparisons are exploratory without multiplicity correction. No settings
were tuned or runs selectively repeated after the comparative timings were seen.

Practical conclusion
--------------------
We have demonstrated a bounded attention execution improvement, not a general
cache-algorithm speedup. Preserve this candidate and its results. The next
separate evaluation should test longer contexts, more KV memory, larger batches
and production execution modes with correctness gates; the native regrouping
path may be preferable elsewhere. Novelty and current-upstream applicability
remain to be established before a research or production claim.
